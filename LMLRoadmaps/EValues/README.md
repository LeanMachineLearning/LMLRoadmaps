# Roadmap: E-values, e-processes and anytime-valid inference

Develop hypothesis testing with e-values: nonnegative statistics whose expectation is at most one
under every distribution of the null hypothesis. Their sequential counterparts, e-processes, stay
valid at every stopping time, so that a statistician may monitor the data, stop or continue at
will, and report a level chosen after seeing the data. The development follows A. Ramdas and
R. Wang, *Hypothesis Testing with E-values* [RW], and the papers it surveys and that followed it.

The literature is recent and its results supersede one another quickly. Each layer therefore
states the strongest result available, often from a 2025–2026 paper or from Statlib's general
theory of the numeraire, and derives the earlier and narrower results from it; those are listed
as corollaries, never as separate targets. The headline results are:
- the characterization of e-processes as the processes dominated by a test supermartingale for
  each null distribution [RRLK, Lemma 6], with Ville's inequality from the exact Ville identity
  [dlP+26], and the composite Ville theorem [RLKR23];
- the identification of numeraires through the effective null [LRR25, Theorem 4.1], which contains
  the log-optimality of likelihood ratios at every σ-algebra and every stopping time;
- the e-variables of nulls generated by finitely many constraints [LRR26, Theorem 3.1] and the dual
  formula $\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}=\max_\pi\mathbb E_Q\log(1+\sum_i\pi_ig_i)$ for every such null on any
  measurable space, of which the bounded, semi-bounded and heavy-tailed $\mathrm K_{\inf}$ formulas
  of the bandit literature are instances;
- the optimal growth rate of e-processes against an i.i.d. composite null, the limit of the
  normalized maximal expected logarithm of `n`-sample e-variables, equal to $\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}$
  under lower semicontinuity [RR26], the characterization of the existence of power-one tests
  against composite alternatives by finite-block events [Ram26], and the bound
  $\mathbb E_Q[\tau_\alpha]\ge\log(1/\alpha)/\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}$ [ARR26];
- testing by betting: against a conditional mean constrained to a convex set, every e-process is
  dominated by a betting wealth [Cle26b]; and confidence sequences by duality;
- merging: the e-merging functions are the functions dominated by weighted means [Wan25; Cle26],
  and the admissible merging functions of sequential e-values are the martingale merging
  functions [VW24];
- multiple testing through dynamic e-closure [dH26], of which fixed-time e-closure [XSF+26], e-BH
  for compound e-values, closed e-BH, closed BH [Goe26], online e-BH and stopped e-BH are
  instances;
- exchangeability, conformal e-prediction, change detection and nonparametric two-sample testing.

Build the surrounding library at the generality of the literature:
- e-variables, e-processes and p-variables for arbitrary sets of measures, with no dominating
  measure, no convexity and no parametrization, valued in `ℝ≥0∞`;
- any filtration indexed by `ℕ`, and data that need not be independent;
- conditional e-variables and test supermartingales stated with set integrals, with no
  integrability side conditions;
- Lagrange duality for finitely many constraints, the essential supremum of a family of functions,
  and the discrete-time Snell envelope.

## Scope and dependencies

This roadmap owns e-values and e-processes: their definitions for composite nulls, their
validity, optimality and growth, their merging, and their use in sequential testing, confidence
sequences, multiple testing, conformal prediction and change detection. It also owns four general
tools that it needs and that no dependency provides: Lagrange duality for convex programs with
finitely many constraints (Layer 1.4), the convergence and optional stopping of `ℝ≥0∞`-valued
supermartingales at arbitrary stopping times (Layer 2.2), the essential supremum of a family of
functions with the discrete-time Snell envelope (Layer 3.1), and first-passage times of random
walks with positive drift (Layer 6.3). It owns the dual formula of $\mathrm K_{\inf}$ for
nonparametric classes (Layer 1.4), which the Identification roadmap's Layer 1.4 defines and does not
compute.

**Statlib.** Statlib, a dependency of LML at LMLExplore's pin, already contains the theory of
e-variables at a fixed sample size: definitions, convexity, the existence of the numeraire for any
class of e-variables (`IsEVarClass`) and any utility, the reverse information projection and its
duality with the Kullback–Leibler divergence for any set of measures, products, and Bernoulli
computations. This roadmap consumes them, does not restate them, and builds on them: the
log-optimality of likelihood ratios, the dual formula of $\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}$ and the optimal
growth rate of e-processes are all derived from Statlib's numeraire and duality.

- e-values and risk measures [RW, Chapter 16], merging of dependent p-values [RW, Chapter 12], and

- the optimality of the sequential probability ratio test [WW48] and the boosted SPRT;
- knockoffs, and the complete classes of FDR procedures with e-values;
- confidence sequences for heavy-tailed, Banach-valued or vector-valued data, PAC-Bayes
  confidence sequences and Huber-robust confidence sequences.

## Conventions

**Sources and numbering.** Statements cite [RW] by the numbering of its sixth arXiv version, which
the fifth shares (the fourth differs), and the original papers by the keys of the References
section with the numbering of the arXiv version listed there. Rounds are numbered from 0.

**Nulls.** A null hypothesis is a set `S : Set (Measure Ω)`, as for Statlib's `IsEVar`; formulas
write it $\mathcal P$ (Mathlib reserves `𝒫` for the power set). Theorems assume
`∀ P ∈ S, IsProbabilityMeasure P`.
- No dominating measure, no convexity and no topology are assumed unless the statement names one
  (likelihood ratios, universal inference, the dominated forms of Layer 1, the weak topology of
  Layer 6.1).
- A parametric null is `Set.range P` for `P : Θ → Measure Ω`.
- A family of nulls indexed by parameters, `S : Θ → Set (Measure Ω)` with `P ∈ S θ` meaning that
  `θ` is a true parameter of `P`, is the input of confidence sequences (Layer 5). A functional
  `φ` on a model `M` is the case `S θ = {P ∈ M | φ P = θ}`; partially identified parameters are
  covered.
- Statements about a composite null hold under each of its members: "almost surely" means
  `P`-almost surely for every `P ∈ S`, and independence is independence under every `P ∈ S`. A
  property holds `S`-quasi-surely when it holds `P`-almost surely for every `P ∈ S`.

**Values.** E-variables, e-processes, test supermartingales, wealth processes and merging
functions are `ℝ≥0∞`-valued, and expectations are lower integrals `∫⁻`.
- Statlib's `IsEVar` is defined through `∫ᵉ X - 1 ≤ 0`; for probability measures, use its
  equivalence with `∫⁻ X ≤ 1` (`IsEVar.of_lintegral_le_one`, `IsEVar.lintegral_le_measure_univ`)
  and state results with `∫⁻`. For a finite measure `μ` it asks `∫⁻ X ∂μ ≤ μ univ`, which is
  stronger than the condition `∫⁻ X ∂μ ≤ 1` defining the effective null; state statements about
  sub-probability measures of the effective null with `∫⁻`.
- P-variables are `ℝ≥0∞`-valued as well, with `P {ω | p ω ≤ α} ≤ α` for every `α : ℝ≥0∞`; values
  above `1` carry no evidence. The e-to-p calibration is `E ↦ E⁻¹`, with `0⁻¹ = ∞` and
  `∞⁻¹ = 0`, and no truncation at `1`.
- Levels are `α : ℝ≥0∞`; an e-test rejects when `α⁻¹ ≤ E`.
- Real nonnegative supermartingales (Mathlib's `Supermartingale`) enter through `ENNReal.ofReal`
  (Layer 2.1).
- Expected logarithms of `ℝ≥0∞`-valued variables are `∫ᵉ ω, ENNReal.log (X ω) ∂Q`, valued in
  `EReal`, as in Statlib's `maxUtility` with `logUtility` and `IsNumeraire.eintegral_log_le`;
  never the Bochner integral, which is `0` for non-integrable functions.

**Conditional statements.** Conditional expectations of `ℝ≥0∞`-valued variables are not used.
"$\mathbb E_P[X\mid m]\le Y$" is stated as
`∀ s, MeasurableSet[m] s → ∫⁻ ω in s, X ω ∂P ≤ ∫⁻ ω in s, Y ω ∂P`, the form of LMLExplore's
`supermartingale_of_setLIntegral_succ_le`. A conditional law is given by a kernel through the same
set-integral identity, or Mathlib's `HasCondDistrib`.

**Time, filtrations and stopping times.**
- Time is `ℕ` and filtrations are arbitrary: `ℱ : Filtration ℕ mΩ`. Observations need not be
  independent, identically distributed or a product. The canonical filtrations of a sequence of
  observations are Mathlib's `Filtration.piLE` on `ℕ → 𝓧` (the observations `0, …, n` at time `n`,
  as in Statlib's `Testing/Sequential/`) and LMLExplore's `Filtration.naturalLT` (the observations
  before `n`); statements hold for every filtration, and statements about i.i.d. data (Layer 6)
  use `Filtration.piLE`, as Statlib's `Testing/Sequential/` does, so that time `n` has seen
  `n + 1` observations.
- A process `E` is adapted when `E n` is `ℱ n`-measurable. The factor of round `n` of a product, a
  bet or a sequential e-variable, is `ℱ (n + 1)`-measurable, and is a conditional e-variable given
  `ℱ n`; bets and portfolios used in round `n` are `ℱ n`-measurable.
- Stopping times are Mathlib's `IsStoppingTime ℱ τ` with `τ : Ω → WithTop ℕ`. A stopping time with
  values in `ℕ` is the coercion `fun ω ↦ (τ ω : WithTop ℕ)`, as in Statlib's
  `Testing/Sequential/`. Mathlib's `stoppedValue` takes an arbitrary value at `⊤`; never rely on
  it. The value of a process at a possibly infinite stopping time is `stoppedValueLimsup E τ`,
  equal to `E (τ ω) ω` where `τ ω` is finite and to `limsup (E · ω)` where it is infinite
  [RRLK; RLKR23, (1.2)].

**Theorem shapes.** Validity statements quantify over every member of the null and every stopping
time. Constants are explicit. Asymptotic statements are eventual inequalities or limits along
`atTop` and `𝓝[>] 0`, as in the Identification roadmap.

**Generality.** Each layer says where its statements generalize the papers. Do not reintroduce
simple nulls, dominated nulls, real-valued e-values, i.i.d. data, bounded observations or finite
horizons as the primary statements where the layer states the general form, and do not prove a
special case listed as a corollary before the general statement: derive it.

**Namespaces and names.** Definitions live in `ProbabilityTheory`, next to Statlib's, with names
in Statlib's pattern: `IsPVar`, `IsCondEVar`, `IsTestSupermartingale`, `IsTestMartingale`,
`IsEProcess`, `IsPProcess`, `IsConfSeq`, `IsEMerging`, `IsCompoundEVar`, `IsECollection`,
`constraintNull`. Statlib's names keep their names.

## Layer 0: E-variables, p-variables, calibration and post-hoc validity

1. **P-variables, tests and Markov's inequality** [RW, Definition 1.2, Fact 1.3, Proposition 2.1].
   - Define `IsPVar p S` for a measurable `p : Ω → ℝ≥0∞`: `P {ω | p ω ≤ α} ≤ α` for every
     `P ∈ S` and every `α : ℝ≥0∞`. Define exact e-variables (`∫⁻ E ∂P = 1` for every `P ∈ S`)
     and exact p-variables (`P {p ≤ α} = α` for `α ≤ 1`).
   - Prove Markov's inequality for e-variables, `P {ω | α⁻¹ ≤ E ω} ≤ α`, hence that `E⁻¹` is a
     p-variable, that the indicator of `{α⁻¹ ≤ E}` has `P`-mass at most `α`, and that
     `min (α * E) 1` has expectation at most `α`.
   - Prove that products of finitely many e-variables that are independent under every `P ∈ S`
     are e-variables; Statlib's `IsEVar.prod` (product measures) is the case of the coordinates
     of a product space.
2. **Calibrators** [RW, Definition 2.3, Propositions 2.4–2.7; VW21, Propositions 2.1–2.2].
   - Define a p-to-e calibrator as an antitone `f : ℝ≥0∞ → ℝ≥0∞`, zero on `(1, ∞]`, with
     `∫⁻ x in Icc 0 1, f (ENNReal.ofReal x) ≤ 1`: [RW, Proposition 2.5]'s characterization is the
     definition. Prove that `f ∘ p` is an e-variable for every p-variable `p` for every null on
     every measurable space, and conversely that an antitone `f` vanishing on `(1, ∞]` for which
     `f` applied to the identity of `[0, 1]` under Lebesgue measure is an e-variable is a
     calibrator.
   - Prove the admissibility characterization (upper semicontinuous, `f 0 = ∞`, integral `1`),
     the rescaling `x ↦ a f (a x)` for `a ≥ 1` [RW, Proposition 2.6], and the instances
     $\kappa x^{\kappa-1}$, $\kappa\in(0,1)$, and their mixture over $\kappa$, with its closed
     form $(1-x+x\log x)/(x(\log x)^2)$ on $(0,1)$.
   - E-to-p: every antitone `g` such that `g ∘ E` is a p-variable for every e-variable `E` on the
     unit interval satisfies `min 1 t⁻¹ ≤ g t`, so `t ↦ min 1 t⁻¹` is the only admissible e-to-p
     calibrator [RW, Proposition 2.4].
   - For an atomless probability measure `P`, every e-variable for `{P}` is `P`-almost surely
     `f ∘ p` for a calibrator `f` and an exact p-variable `p`, with `f` admissible when the
     e-variable is exact [RW, Proposition 2.7].
3. **The randomized Markov inequality** [RW, Theorem 2.9, Corollary 2.10; RM26, Theorem 1.2].
   For a measurable `Y : Ω → ℝ≥0∞` and `U` uniform on `[0, 1]` and independent of `Y` under a
   probability measure `P`, prove `P {U ≤ α * Y} = ∫⁻ min (α * Y) 1 ∂P ≤ α * ∫⁻ Y ∂P`. Deduce
   that `p / E` is a p-variable for a p-variable `p` and an e-variable `E` that are independent
   under every member of the null, and that `U / E` is one.
4. **Post-hoc validity** [RW, Definition 4.1, Propositions 4.3–4.4].
   - For a family of tests `φ : ℝ≥0∞ → Ω → ℝ≥0∞` indexed by the levels in `(0, 1)`, with values in
     `[0, 1]`, define post-hoc validity: `∫⁻ ω, ⨆ α ∈ Ioo 0 1, φ α ω / α ∂P ≤ 1` for every `P ∈ S`.
     Prove its equivalence with `∫⁻ φ (α̂ ω) ω / α̂ ω ∂P ≤ 1` for every measurable random level
     `α̂ : Ω → Ioo 0 1`, for families nondecreasing in the level, for which the supremum is over
     the rational levels and no measurable selection is needed.
   - Prove that `fun α ω ↦ min (α * E ω) 1` and the indicators of `{α⁻¹ ≤ E}` are post-hoc valid
     for every e-variable `E`, and that every post-hoc valid family nondecreasing in the level is
     dominated by `min (α * E) 1`, with `E = ⨆ α, φ α / α` an e-variable [RW, Proposition 4.3].
   - Corollary: `p` is a post-hoc p-variable (`∫⁻ ⨆ α ∈ Ioo 0 1, 𝟙{p ≤ α} / α ≤ 1`) exactly when
     `p⁻¹`, truncated to `0` on `{1 ≤ p}`, is an e-variable [RW, Proposition 4.4], which is
     [Kon23, Theorem 2] and the e-variable compatibility of [Gru24, Proposition 1].
5. **Admissibility** [RW, Definition 2.11, Theorem 2.14, Corollary 2.15, Propositions 2.12, 2.13
   and 2.17].
   - Define an admissible e-variable: every e-variable `E' ≥ E` equals `E` almost surely under
     every member of the null.
   - Prove the characterization of [RW, Theorem 2.14]: `E` is admissible exactly when
     $\liminf_{\delta\downarrow0}\inf\{(1-\mathbb E_P[E])/\delta : P\in\mathcal P,\ P(A)>\delta\}=0$
     for every measurable `A`, with `⨅ ∅ = 0`. Derive from it its sufficient conditions [RW, Corollary 2.15], the necessary
     condition [RW, Proposition 2.13], and for a simple null `{P}` that admissible and exact
     coincide, with every exact e-variable `P`-almost surely `S.rnDeriv P` for a probability
     measure `S ≪ P` [RW, Proposition 2.12].
   - Call `E` strongly admissible when every measurable `A` charged by some member of the null is
     charged by some `P ∈ S` with `∫⁻ E ∂P = 1` [RW, Corollary 2.15(a)]. Products of strongly
     admissible e-variables are strongly admissible for the product null [RW, Proposition 2.17].

Generalization: arbitrary sets of measures, where [VW21] states calibrators for one probability
space at a time; `ℝ≥0∞`-valued p-variables, so that `E⁻¹` needs no truncation.

## Layer 1: Optimal e-variables

Statlib provides the numeraire for any set of measures, any finite measure and any utility, its
uniqueness and optimality, and the duality
`maxUtility P S logUtility = ⨅ μ ∈ effectiveSet S, klIntegral P μ`. This layer identifies
numeraires, describes the effective null, and computes both sides of the duality for the classes
of nulls of the literature.

1. **The effective null** [LRR25, Definition 3.1, Lemma 4.5, Corollary 4.6; RW, §6.3.1,
   Theorem 6.10].
   - For Statlib's `effectiveSet S`, prove: it contains every mixture `κ ∘ₘ π` of a probability
     measure `π` and a kernel `κ` with values in `S`, hence the convex hull of `S`; it is solid

     Theorem 3.1], `ν A = 0` exactly when some e-process tends to `∞` on `A`, by mixing the
     e-processes for `ε = 4⁻ᵏ` with weights `2⁻ᵏ`.
   - The truncated-Cauchy example [RLKR23, Example 2.5] has `sup P (A) = 0` and `ν A = 1` [RLKR23,
     Proposition 2.6].
6. **Fork-convexity** [RRLK22, Definitions 10–11, Lemma 12, Theorems 13–14, Corollary 16,
   Theorem 17].
   - Define fork-convex combinations and the closed fork-convex hull of a locally dominated null
     with respect to a reference measure, and prove that a process that is a nonnegative
     supermartingale for every member of `S` is one for every member of the closed fork-convex
     hull [RRLK22, Theorem 13].
   - For a locally dominated fork-convex null, the composite Snell envelope is the smallest process
     dominating `E` that is a nonnegative supermartingale for every member [RRLK22, Theorem 14];
     an e-process is dominated by a single process that is a supermartingale for every member
     exactly when it is an e-process for the closed fork-convex hull [RRLK22, Corollary 16].
   - For binary observations, the closed fork-convex hull of the i.i.d. Bernoulli laws is the set
     of all laws, so that every process that is a nonnegative supermartingale under all i.i.d.
     Bernoulli laws is nonincreasing [RRLK22, Theorem 17]. This is the standard example of
     e-processes (Layer 9.1) that no single test supermartingale dominates.
7. **Admissibility** [RRLK, Theorem 18, Proposition 9, Corollaries 26 and 29, Theorem 35].
   - Define admissibility as [RRLK, §4.2] does: `E'` dominates `E` when `P (E n ≤ E' n) = 1` for
     every `n` and `P ∈ S`, strictly when moreover `0 < P (E n < E' n)` for some `n` and `P ∈ S`;
     `E` is admissible when no e-process dominates it strictly. For a locally dominated null,
     every e-process is dominated by an admissible one [RRLK, Proposition 9], and every admissible
     e-process is the essential infimum over `P ∈ S` of `P`-martingales with initial expectation
     `1` [RRLK, Corollary 26]. Derive the simple-null case, where the admissible e-processes are the test
     martingales with initial expectation `1` [RRLK, Theorem 18]. Composite nonnegative
     martingales with initial expectation `1` are admissible [RRLK, Corollary 29].
   - Instance: `exp (∑ k < n, X k - n / 2)` is admissible for the null of conditionally
     `1`-sub-Gaussian increments [RRLK, Theorem 35].

Generalization: arbitrary nulls with no domination and arbitrary filtrations, where [RW] defines
e-processes on the natural filtration; validity at every stopping time with the value
`stoppedValueLimsup` at infinity, where [RW] leaves the value at infinity undefined.

## Layer 4: Constructions of e-processes

1. **Likelihood ratio processes** [RW, §7.5; RRLK, Proposition 4].
   - For probability measures `P` and `Q` and a filtration `ℱ`, the process
     `n ↦ (Q.trim (ℱ.le n)).rnDeriv (P.trim (ℱ.le n))` is a test supermartingale for `P`, and a
     test martingale when `Q.trim (ℱ.le n) ≪ P.trim (ℱ.le n)` for every `n`. Its log-optimality at
     stopping times is Layer 1.2.
   - For i.i.d. observations and `Filtration.piLE` it is $\prod_{i\le n}\frac{dQ_1}{dP_1}(X_i)$
     (Statlib's `rnDeriv_trim_piLE_infinitePi`).
   - Conversely, on `ℕ → 𝓧` with `𝓧` standard Borel and the canonical filtration, every test
     martingale for a probability measure `P` with initial value `1` is the likelihood ratio
     process of some probability measure against `P`, built from the conditional densities
     `M (n + 1) / M n` with Mathlib's Ionescu–Tulcea `Kernel.traj` [RRLK, Proposition 4].
2. **Sequential predictors and their regret** [RW, §3.7, §6.6, §7.5, Definition 7.13,
   Proposition 7.14; HRMS21, Lemma 2; Rob70].
   - Let `X k` be `ℱ (k + 1)`-measurable observations in a measurable space `𝓧`, and let
     `κ k ω` be the conditional law of `X k` given `ℱ k` under `P`: for every `g : Ω → 𝓧 → ℝ≥0∞`
     measurable for `ℱ k` and `𝓧` jointly and every `ℱ k`-measurable `s`,
     `∫⁻ ω in s, g ω (X k ω) ∂P = ∫⁻ ω in s, ∫⁻ x, g ω x ∂(κ k ω) ∂P`. Let the prediction of
     round `k` have the jointly measurable density `r k ω` with respect to `κ k ω`, with
     `∫⁻ x, r k ω x ∂(κ k ω) ≤ 1`. Prove that $\prod_{k<n}r_k(X_k)$ is a test supermartingale
     for `P`, and a test martingale when the predictions are probability measures. For
     predictions given as `ℱ k`-measurable sub-Markov kernels on a countably generated `𝓧`, the
     density is Mathlib's `Kernel.rnDeriv`.
   - This is the restatement of `LMLExplore.predictorLikelihoodRatio` (Restatements of merged
     code): a finite alphabet with `κ k = ν` is the special case.
   - **Regret.** For a family of alternatives with conditional densities, the logarithm of the
     predictor's likelihood ratio equals that of every fixed alternative minus the log-loss regret
     of the predictor against it, pathwise. Deduce that a predictor whose expected regret against
     every alternative of a class is `o(n)` is asymptotically log-optimal against the class in the
     sense of [RW, Definition 7.13], and derive [RW, Proposition 7.14] (plug-in alternatives with
     log-concave likelihoods) by bounding the regret of the plug-in predictor.
   - **Mixtures.** For a kernel `θ ↦ Q θ` of alternatives with
     `(Q θ).trim (ℱ.le n) ≪ P.trim (ℱ.le n)` for every `θ` and `n`, and a prior `π`, the mixture
     over `π` of their likelihood ratio processes is the likelihood ratio process of the mixture,
     that of the predictor of the posterior predictive laws, so that Bayes factors with a simple
     null are test martingales [HRMS21, Lemma 2; Rob70], with the regret of the Gibbs mixtures of
     OnlineLearning 10. Without the absolute continuity the mixture is a test supermartingale. The
     Krichevsky–Trofimov and Laplace mixtures (LMLExplore's `InformationTheory/`) are instances.
     A Bayes factor with a prior on a composite null is in general not an e-variable for the null:
     give the example of [RW, §6.6].
3. **Universal inference** [WRB20, Theorems 1 and 9–11; RW, Theorem 5.1, Propositions 5.3 and
   7.18].
   - Let every member `P` of the null be locally dominated by `μ` (`P.trim (ℱ.le n) ≪
     μ.trim (ℱ.le n)` for every `n`), let `p̂ n` be `ℱ n`-measurable and `μ`-almost everywhere at
     least the density of every member on `ℱ n` (a null maximum likelihood), and, on
     `Ω = ℕ → 𝓧` with its canonical filtration, let `q n` be the density on `ℱ n` of the law of a
     sequential predictor, the trajectory `Kernel.traj` of its prediction kernels (item 2). Prove that `q n / p̂ n` is an
     e-process for `S`: it is dominated by the likelihood ratio process of the predictor against
     each member (Layers 3.2 and 4.1).
   - Derive the split likelihood ratio e-variable [WRB20, Theorem 1; RW, Theorem 5.1] as the case
     of one block fitted on independent data, averages over splits with their e-power [RW,
     Proposition 5.3], and the anytime-valid p-value and universal confidence sequence [WRB20,
     Theorems 10–11].
4. **Betting against conditional constraints** [Cle26b, Theorem 1, Proposition 2, Corollary 1;
   WSR24, Proposition 2; Cle25, Theorems 2–3; LRR26, §10; RW, §7.6, Algorithms 7.16–7.17].
   - For finitely many measurable `g : ι → 𝓧 → ℝ` and `ℱ (k + 1)`-measurable observations `X k`,
     define the sequential constraint null: the laws under which every `g i (X k)` is integrable
     and $\mathbb E[g_i(X_k)\mid\mathcal F_k]\le0$, in the set-integral form. Prove that the
     betting wealth $\prod_{k<n}(1+\sum_i\pi_{k,i}g_i(X_k))$, for `ℱ k`-measurable bets
     `π k ∈ Π g` (Layer 1.4), is a test supermartingale for every member, as a case of the
     portfolios of Layer 2.1.
   - **The complete class** [Cle26b, Theorem 1]. For a standard Borel space `𝓧`, a Borel statistic
     `Φ : 𝓧 → ℝ^m`, a nonempty convex `S ⊆ ℝ^m` (not necessarily closed or Borel), and
     `Ω = ℕ → 𝓧` with the filtration of the past observations, consider the null of the laws under
     which `Φ (X k)` is integrable and its conditional mean given the past lies in `S`. Prove that a
     nonnegative adapted process with `E 0 ≤ 1` is an e-process for this null exactly when it is
     majorized, pointwise and at every time, by a predictable product of the affine one-step
     e-variables of Layer 1.4 (Borel bets `λ k` of the past observations, the factor `∞` off the
     effective domain, with Clerico's convention `0 * ∞ = ∞` there). In particular test
     supermartingales lose nothing. The proof builds the bets by induction over the horizon from
     a one-step separation argument [Cle26b, Lemma 4].
   - Restricting the bets to those whose affine e-variables are maximal gives the minimal complete
     class [Cle26b, Proposition 2]; for equality constraints (`S` a point) every affine product is
     already maximal [Cle26b, Corollary 1].
   - The inequality constraints `g i ≤ 0` are the case `Φ = g`, `S = (-∞, 0]^ι`, with the bets
     `1 + ∑ i, π i * g i`. Derive, with no separate proofs: one mean on a Borel subset of `[0, 1]`
     containing `0` and `1` [Cle25, Theorem 3], its finite-horizon form [Cle25, Theorem 2], and
     that every test martingale for a constant conditional mean with initial value `1` is a
     betting wealth [WSR24, Proposition 2]. This answers the question of [LRR26, §10] for finitely
     many constraints.
   - The betting martingale of OnlineLearning 16.4 is the case of one mean constraint on `[0, 1]`
     with equality; the per-round log-optimal bets are the numeraires of Layer 1.4 for the
     conditional laws of the alternative (item 5).
5. **Sequential numeraires** [GdHK24, Proposition 2, Corollary 1; KG22, Theorem 12].
   - For a set `S₁` of probability measures on `𝓧`, define the sequential null of the laws under
     which the conditional law of each observation `X k` given `ℱ k` lies in `S₁`; it contains the
     laws of i.i.d. sequences with law in `S₁` and of independent sequences with laws in `S₁`, and
     the sequential constraint null of item 4 is the case `S₁ = constraintNull Φ`.
   - For a probability measure `Q₁` on `𝓧`, prove that $\prod_{k<n}X^*(X_k)$, with
     `X* = numeraire Q₁ S₁` (Statlib), is a test supermartingale for every member of the
     sequential null.

Generalization: any filtration and any conditional law of the observations, where LMLExplore's
predictor ratios use a finite alphabet and independent observations of one law; composite nulls
through domination by test supermartingale families; the complete class for a conditional mean of
any finite-dimensional statistic in any convex set [Cle26b], where [WSR24] and [Cle25] treat a mean
on `[0, 1]`.

## Layer 5: Confidence sequences

1. **Definition and coverage at random times** [RRLK, §4.3, Lemma 2; HRMS21, Lemma 3; RW,
   Definition 13.3; DR67].
   - For a type `Θ` of parameters and nulls `S : Θ → Set (Measure Ω)`, define `IsConfSeq C ℱ S α`
     for `C : ℕ → Ω → Set Θ` with `{ω | θ ∈ C n ω}` `ℱ n`-measurable for every `θ`:
     `P {ω | ∃ n, θ ∉ C n ω} ≤ α` for every `θ` and every `P ∈ S θ`.
   - Prove its equivalence with coverage at every random time, with `C ∞ = liminf C n`, and at
     every stopping time (Layer 3.2); prove that running intersections of confidence sequences
     are confidence sequences.
2. **Duality** [RRLK, Proposition 13, Theorem 30; WSR24, Theorem 1; RW, §13.1].
   - For e-processes `E θ` for `S θ`, `{θ | E θ n < α⁻¹}` is a confidence sequence; this contains
     [WSR24, Theorem 1], whose hypothesis is the domination of Layer 3.1.
   - Conversely, every confidence sequence is of this form for the e-processes
     `α⁻¹ * 𝟙{∃ k ≤ n, θ ∉ C k}`, and admissible confidence sequences correspond to families of
     admissible tests [RRLK, Theorem 30].
   - Universal confidence sequences from Layer 4.3.
3. **Sub-ψ processes, line crossings and stitching** [HRMS20, Definition 1, Theorem 1; HRMS21,
   Definition 1, Theorem 1].
   - Define sub-ψ processes `(S, V)` with initial bound `l₀`: for each `λ ∈ [0, λ_max)` some
     supermartingale `L λ ≥ exp (λ S - ψ λ V)` with `L λ 0 ≤ l₀` [HRMS20, Definition 1]. The
     exponential supermartingales of Identification 4.2 and Freedman's (LMLExplore's
     `Probability/Martingale/Freedman.lean`) are instances; by [LRR26, Theorem 4.3] (Layer 1.4)
     mixtures over `λ` are the complete class of e-variables for the one-step sub-ψ null.
   - Prove the line-crossing inequality
     $P(\exists n:\ S_n\ge a+bV_n)\le l_0e^{-aD(b)}$ for `a, b > 0`, with the decay transform
     $D(b)=\sup\{\lambda\in[0,\lambda_{\max}):\psi(\lambda)\le b\lambda\}$ (`0` for the empty set),
     valued in `ℝ≥0∞` [HRMS20, Theorem 1(a)], its equivalent forms for CGF-like `ψ`
     [HRMS20, Theorem 1(b)–(d)], and the stitched boundary of [HRMS21, Theorem 1], of order
     $\sqrt{V\log\log V}$.

Generalization: parameters of any type with set-valued truth, where [HRMS21] and [WSR24] state
confidence sequences for a real functional.

## Layer 6: Growth rates, power and expected stopping times

The data are the coordinates of `ℕ → 𝓧` with the filtration `Filtration.piLE`, so that time `n`
has seen `n + 1` observations, as in Statlib's `Testing/Sequential/`; the alternative is
`Q = Measure.infinitePi (fun _ ↦ Q₁)` and the null contains the i.i.d. laws of `S₁`. Growth rates
are per observation. Write
$\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}(Q_1,\mathcal P_1)=\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_1}\mathrm{KL}(Q_1,P)$ in `ℝ≥0∞`, and
`a n = maxUtility (Measure.pi (fun _ : Fin n ↦ Q₁)) {Measure.pi (fun _ ↦ P) | P ∈ S₁} logUtility`,
the maximal expected logarithm of an `n`-sample e-variable, which Statlib's duality writes as the
reverse information projection of `Q₁^{⊗n}` onto the effective null of the `n`-sample null.

1. **The optimal growth rate** [RR26, Proposition 3.1, Lemmas 3.2–3.3, Theorem 3.5, Theorem 4.1,
   Propositions 4.5, 5.4 and 7.1].
   - Blockwise e-processes: for an increasing sequence of times `t k`, e-processes for the
     filtration `k ↦ ℱ (t k)`, lifted to `ℱ` [RR26, Definitions 2.3–2.5].
   - Prove that `a` is superadditive, from Statlib's `IsEVar.prod` [RR26, Lemma 3.2], so that
     `a n / n` converges to its supremum `d`; that every e-process and every blockwise e-process
     `W` has `limsup (1/n) ∫ᵉ ω, ENNReal.log (W n ω) ∂Q ≤ d` per observation, since `W n` is an
     e-variable of the observations it has seen [RR26,
     Lemma 3.3]; and that products over blocks of length `m` of Statlib's `numeraire` of the
     `m`-sample null attain every rate below `d` [RR26, Theorem 3.5]. No assumption is made on
     `S₁` or `Q₁`.
   - Prove `d ≤ KL_inf(Q₁, S₁)`, and `d = KL_inf(Q₁, S₁)` when `𝓧` is Polish and `R ↦ KL_inf(R, S₁)`
     is lower semicontinuous at `Q₁` for the weak topology, in particular when `S₁` is weakly
     compact [RR26, Theorem 4.1]; then a test supermartingale on blocks has `(1/t k) log` tending
     to `KL_inf(Q₁, S₁)` almost surely and in expectation [RR26, Proposition 7.1]. The inequality
     can be strict [RR26, Proposition 5.4]; for `S₁` weakly compact and convex,
     `a 1 = KL_inf(Q₁, S₁)` [RR26, Proposition 4.5].
   - For the sequential null of Layer 4.5, `a n = n * maxUtility Q₁ S₁ logUtility` (Statlib's
     `maxUtility_prod_eq_add`), and the product of the numeraires has
     `(1/n) log (E n) → maxUtility Q₁ S₁ logUtility` almost surely when
     $\mathbb E_Q|\log X^*(X_1)|<\infty$ (`strong_law_ae`). For a constraint null with Slater's
     condition this rate is $\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}$ (Layer 1.4).
   - Almost surely, every e-process `E` for the null has `limsup (1/n) log (E n) ≤ KL_inf(Q₁, S₁)`:
     by Layer 3.1 it is dominated by a test supermartingale for each `P₁`-i.i.d. law, whose ratio to
     the likelihood ratio process of Layer 4.1 is a nonnegative supermartingale under `Q`, and the
     strong law gives the rate of the likelihood ratio [WSJ26, Proposition 2.2, for portfolio
     nulls].
2. **Power one** [Ram26, Theorem 3.1, Corollaries 3.2 and 3.6; RR26, Theorem 3.14, Lemma 3.9; RW,
   Proposition 3.12, Theorem 3.14].
   - Prove [Ram26, Theorem 3.1] for i.i.d. data on any measurable space, a null `S₁` and a set of
     alternatives `T₁`: the following are equivalent. For some (equivalently every) `α ∈ (0, 1)`,
     a level-`α` sequential test has power one against every alternative; some e-process for the
     null tends to `∞` almost surely under every alternative (or has `limsup` equal to `∞`); a
     countable family of finite-block events `B m ⊆ 𝓧^{n m}` has
     `sup P ∈ S₁, P^{⊗ n m} (B m) < Q₁^{⊗ n m} (B m)` for some `m`, for every alternative `Q₁`;
     the same with randomized fixed-sample tests, with bounded scores of nonpositive null
     expectation, or with bounded estimable functionals; and a test supermartingale on blocks
     tends to `∞` under every alternative.
   - The tests can be chosen with `E_Q[τ_α] ≤ A_Q + B_Q log(1/α)` [Ram26, Corollary 3.2], and for a
     simple null one-observation events suffice [Ram26, Corollary 3.6].
   - Derive [RR26, Theorem 3.14], the case of one alternative: power one holds exactly when
     `Q₁^{⊗k}` lies outside the effective null of the `k`-sample null for some `k`, exactly when
     `a k > 0` for some `k`, exactly when `d > 0` (finite-block events against the effective null,
     through Layer 1.1).
   - Derive the consistency of products of i.i.d. e-variables with positive e-power and their
     convergence to `0` with negative e-power [RW, Proposition 3.12]; and `1 < ∫⁻ X ∂Q` exactly
     when $\mathbb E_Q\log(1-\lambda+\lambda X)>0$ for some $\lambda\in[0,1]$ [RW,
     Theorem 3.14].
3. **Expected stopping times** [ARR26, Theorems 3.1 and 4.1; WSJ26, Theorems 2.3 and 3.3,
   Corollary 2.4; Gut09].
   - Lower bound: for every level-`α` sequential test `τ` for the null (`P (τ < ∞) ≤ α` for the
     i.i.d. laws of `S₁`) with `Q (τ < ∞) = 1`, the expected number of observations satisfies
     $\mathbb E_Q[\tau+1]\ge\log(1/\alpha)/\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}(Q_1,\mathcal P_1)$, from
     $\mathbb E_Q[\tau\wedge N+1]\,\mathrm{KL}(Q_1,P)\ge\mathrm{kl}(Q(\tau\le N),P(\tau\le N))$
     for each `P ∈ S₁` and each horizon `N` (Statlib's `klDiv_trim_stoppingTime`, which takes
     stopping times with values in `ℕ`, for `τ ∧ N`, and the data processing inequality on the
     event `{τ ≤ N}`), letting `N → ∞`. For a family `(τ α)` of level-`α` tests, prove the almost
     sure form `liminf (τ (exp (-n)) / n) ≥ 1 / KL_inf(Q₁, S₁)` `Q`-almost surely [ARR26,
     Theorem 3.1].
   - The lower bound is not attained in general: for the null of [RR26, Proposition 5.4],
     $\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}>1/2$ and no power-one test exists (item 2).
   - **First passage times.** For i.i.d. real increments with mean $D\in(0,\infty)$, the first time
     the partial sums reach `a` has expectation equivalent to `a / D` as `a → ∞` [Gut09]. Tau
     Ceti's elementary renewal theorem covers nonnegative increments; prove the signed case.
   - Upper bound: an e-process whose logarithm grows almost surely at the rate
     $\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}$, with the integrability of [ARR26, Theorem 4.1], attains the lower
     bound as `α → 0` [ARR26, Theorem 4.1]. Derive with first passage times the case of the
     product of the numeraires of Layer 4.5 when its rate (item 1) is $\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}$.
   - **Regret.** For portfolio e-processes (Layer 2.1) against i.i.d. vectors of e-variables with
     one coordinate identically `1` (the null class of [WSJ26, (2)]), the log-wealth is that of the
     best constant portfolio minus the regret, pathwise; portfolios with regret `o(n)` have
     `(1/n) log (W n) → ℓ*_Q` almost surely [WSJ26, Theorem 2.3] and
     $\mathbb E_Q[\tau_\alpha]/\log(1/\alpha)\to1/\ell^*_Q$ under the moment condition of
     [WSJ26, Theorem 3.3], where $\ell^*_Q=\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}$ by Layer 1.4. Cover's universal
     portfolio (OnlineLearning 11.4) is an instance [WSJ26, Corollary 2.4], and for one mean
     constraint on `[0, 1]` the universal portfolio with `k = 2` on the assets `1` and `X / m`,
     whose weights cover the bets in `[0, 1/m]`. The Krichevsky–Trofimov bettor (OnlineLearning
     13.2) is universal only for `{0, 1}`-valued observations: for constant coins `1/2` its regret
     against the best constant bet is linear.

Generalization: arbitrary i.i.d. composite nulls with no domination, convexity or compactness, for
the growth rate and the existence of power-one tests [RR26], where [RW, Proposition 7.12] and
[WSJ26] treat a simple null and portfolio nulls.

## Layer 8: Multiple testing

**Setting.** Hypotheses are indexed by a type `ι`, with nulls `H : ι → Set (Measure Ω)` and the
set of true nulls `N P = {i | P ∈ H i}` under a probability measure `P`. A rejection set is a map
`R : Ω → Finset ι` with `{ω | i ∈ R ω}` measurable for every `i`; a candidate family is a map
`Ω → Set (Finset ι)`, from which any set may be selected after seeing the data. The false discovery
proportion of `R` is `#(R ∩ N) / max #R 1` in `ℝ≥0∞`. Guarantees hold for every probability
measure `P` on `Ω`, with no assumption on the dependence of the e-values.

1. **Dynamic e-closure** [dH26, Definitions 2.1–2.8, Theorems 3.6–3.7 and 3.10–3.12, Remark 3.8].
   - Hypotheses arrive over time: `I t` is the finite set of hypotheses active at time `t`, and a
     candidate family process `C t ⊆ 2^(I t)` is adapted. Define dynamic intersection e-process
     collections `E S`, an e-process for the intersection null of every nonempty finite `S`, their
     future-extension coherence (`E (S ∩ I t) t ≤ E S t`) and time monotonicity, simultaneous
     stopped FDR and simultaneous SupFDR, and the closure candidate family
     `{R ⊆ I t | ∀ S, FDP_S R ≤ α * E S t}`.
   - Prove the dynamic e-closure principle: for a coherent collection the closure controls
     simultaneous stopped FDR at every almost surely finite stopping time [dH26, Theorem 3.6]; for
     a coherent and time-monotone collection it controls simultaneous SupFDR and is setwise
     persistent [dH26, Theorem 3.7]; and conversely every candidate family process that controls
     simultaneous stopped FDR, or SupFDR, is contained in the closure of such a collection [dH26,
     Theorems 3.10–3.11]. Prove the same for bounded losses monotone in the true nulls and local
     in the rejection set [dH26, Theorem 3.12], which include the family-wise error.
   - **Fixed time and general losses** [XSF+26, Definitions 1, 2 and 5, Lemma 4, Theorem 6,
     Corollary 7, Theorems 12, 14 and 48]. For finitely many hypotheses at one time, a loss
     `f : Finset ι → Finset ι → ℝ≥0∞` with `f N ∅ = 0` and `f ∅ R = 0` (possibly unbounded, as the
     number of false discoveries), and an e-collection (`e : Finset ι → Ω → ℝ≥0∞`, `e ∅ = 0`,
     `∫⁻ e (N P) ∂P ≤ 1` for every `P`), prove [XSF+26, Theorem 6]: a candidate family controls `f`
     simultaneously at level `α` (`∫⁻ ⨆ R ∈ 𝓡 ω, f (N P) R ∂P ≤ α`, equivalently at every
     data-dependent choice [XSF+26, Lemma 4]) exactly when it is contained in
     `{R | ∀ S, f S R ≤ α * e S ω}` for some e-collection. For bounded monotone local losses it is
     the case of a single time and a fixed set of hypotheses of item 1 [dH26, Remark 3.8]. Derive the
     classical closure principle for the family-wise error rate [XSF+26, Theorem 12,
     Corollary 13], the FDR form [XSF+26, Theorem 14] and the post-hoc form at data-dependent
     levels, for an e-collection that does not depend on the level [XSF+26, Theorem 48].
2. **Compound e-values and e-BH** [IWR, Definition 2.1, Theorems 2.3, 4.3 and 4.4; WR22,
   Propositions 1–2, Theorem 2, §5.1; RW, Definitions 9.1, 9.8–9.9, Theorems 9.11 and 9.18–9.19,
   Proposition 9.16; XSF+26, Proposition 26].
   - For finitely many hypotheses, `K = Fintype.card ι`, define `IsCompoundEVar E H`:
     `∑ i ∈ N P, ∫⁻ E i ∂P ≤ K` for every `P`. Unfolding the definitions, `S ↦ ∑ i ∈ S, E i / K`
     is an e-collection exactly when `E` is compound, and every self-consistent set `R` has
     `FDP_N R ≤ α * ∑ i ∈ N, E i / K`; e-variables for the `H i` and calibrated compound
     p-variables are compound [IWR, Theorem 2.3].
   - Derive from item 1, with no separate proofs: every self-consistent rejection set
     (`K ≤ α * #R * E i` for `i ∈ R`) has false discovery rate at most
     `(α / K) * ∑ i ∈ N P, ∫⁻ E i ∂P ≤ α`, with its post-hoc form over all levels [RW,
     Theorem 9.11; IWR, Theorem 4.3], and `α * #(N P) / K` for e-variables [WR22, Proposition 2];
     e-BH, with its threshold form [WR22, Proposition 1], the largest self-consistent set and the
     union of all of them [WR22, Theorem 2]; structured and post-selection e-BH [WR22, §5.1]; the Benjamini–Hochberg–
     Yekutieli procedure as e-BH applied to the calibrated p-variables `T (α / (ℓ_K * p i)) / α`
     [RW, Proposition 9.16].
   - Universality: every rejection procedure with false discovery rate at most `α` is e-BH applied
     to the compound e-variables `K * 𝟙{i ∈ R} / (α * max #R 1)` [IWR, Theorem 4.4; RW,
     Theorems 9.18–9.19].
3. **Closed e-BH, closed BH, closed testing for the family-wise error rate and online e-BH**
   [XSF+26, Theorem 17, Lemma 52; RW, Definition 9.31, Theorem 9.32; Goe26, Theorems 5, 8, 12
   and 13; HL25, Proposition 2.2, Theorems 2.3–2.5; VW21, Theorem 5.1; VW23; XFR26, Theorem 1;
   FXR26, Proposition 2.1, Theorem 2.2, Proposition 2.3; XR24, Theorem 1; WDR25, Theorem 2.4;
   FR25, Theorem 3.1].
   - Instances of item 1 with the mean e-collection `S ↦ ∑ i ∈ S, E i / #S`: closed e-BH, which
     controls the false discovery rate by [XSF+26, Theorem 14] and whose candidate sets contain
     every self-consistent set, so that it improves e-BH uniformly [XSF+26, Theorem 17 and its
     proof], with a largest set among the top-ranked hypotheses [XSF+26, Lemma 52];
     with the family-wise error loss, the e-closed test, strong control at data-dependent levels,
     at stopping times and uniformly in time [HL25, Proposition 2.2, Theorems 2.3–2.5], the
     family-wise valid adjustments of [VW21, Theorem 5.1] and the true-discovery bounds of [VW23].
   - Instances of the dynamic e-closure with fixed evidence (`I t = [t]`, the increasing
     e-collections of [XFR26]): the online SupFDR e-closure principle [XFR26, Theorem 1; dH26,
     Remark 3.8], online e-BH [FXR26, Proposition 2.1, Theorem 2.2] and e-LOND [FXR26,
     Proposition 2.3; XR24, Theorem 1]; with fixed hypotheses and evolving evidence, e-BH applied
     at a stopping time to e-processes for a common filtration [WDR25, Theorem 2.4].
   - Closed BH [Goe26]: define PRDS (positive regression dependence on the subset of true nulls)
     and prove the lemma of Blanchard and Roquain that `E[𝟙{p i ≤ f p} / f p] ≤ 1` for valid
     p-variables under PRDS and coordinatewise nonincreasing `f` [Goe26, Theorem 5]; deduce that
     the e-collection built from the p-variables in [Goe26, (9)] is an e-collection under PRDS
     [Goe26, Theorem 8], so that its e-closure, closed BH, controls the false discovery rate
     [Goe26, Theorem 12] and never rejects fewer hypotheses than BH, with the level retained when
     the condition fails [Goe26, Theorem 13].
   - The online closed procedure SeqE-Guard for sequential e-variables controls the true
     discoveries uniformly in time [FR25, Theorem 3.1].
4. **False coverage rate** [RW, Definitions 13.1 and 13.6, Theorem 13.7; XWR24]. Define
   e-confidence intervals `{θ | E θ < α⁻¹}`, level-free when `E` does not depend on `α`
   [RW, Definition 13.1], and the e-BY procedure [RW, Definition 13.6], and prove that for
   level-free e-confidence intervals it controls the false coverage rate of the selected intervals
   under arbitrary dependence and every selection rule, with its post-hoc form over all levels
   [RW, Theorem 13.7].

Generalization: hypotheses arriving over time with evidence evolving at arbitrary stopping times
[dH26], one null per hypothesis, each a set of measures, compound e-variables, and losses of any
form [XSF+26], where [WR22] treats e-variables and the false discovery rate at one time.

## Layer 9: Exchangeability, conformal e-prediction, change detection and two-sample testing

1. **Testing exchangeability** [LRR26, Theorem 5.5, Example 5.7; RRLK22, Theorems 6, 7 and 17;
   SR24, Theorem 2.1; Vov21].
   - At a fixed sample size, with Tau Ceti's `ExchangeableAt`: the e-variables for the exchangeable
     laws are those dominated by $1+f-\bar f$ with $f\ge-1$ and $\bar f\le0$ (Layer 1.4,
     [LRR26, Example 5.7]); the e-variables $f/\bar f$ of nonnegative $f$, with `0 / 0 = 1`, are
     the conformal and soft-rank e-values of item 2.
   - Sequentially, with Tau Ceti's `Exchangeable`: no process other than a nonincreasing one is a
     nonnegative supermartingale for all exchangeable laws of binary sequences in their natural
     filtration (Layer 3.6); the e-process of [RRLK22, Theorem 6] for exchangeability against
     Markov alternatives and its growth rate [RRLK22, Theorem 7]; the pairwise-betting test
     martingale in the filtration of pairs, and its growth rate for binary data under Markov
     alternatives [SR24, Theorem 2.1].
   - Conformal test martingales [Vov21]: the smoothed conformal p-values of an exchangeable
     sequence are i.i.d. uniform on `[0, 1]`, so that products of calibrated conformal p-values are
     test martingales in the filtration they generate.
2. **Conformal e-prediction** [Vov25, Propositions 2 and 11; BB24, Theorem 1; GBJ25, Theorem 2.5,
   Corollary 2.6, Proposition 3.3].
   - Define nonconformity e-measures (permutation-equivariant scores with average at most `1`, and
     their normalization) and the conformal e-predictor. Derive its validity for exchangeable data
     [Vov25, Proposition 2], the soft-rank e-value of exchangeable nonnegative scores [BB24,
     Theorem 1] and the validity of cross-conformal e-predictors [Vov25, Proposition 11] from
     item 1.
   - Prediction sets at data-dependent levels have post-hoc coverage (Layer 0.4) [GBJ25,
     Proposition 3.3], and products over batches exchangeable given the past batches are test
     supermartingales (Layer 2.1), with anytime-valid prediction sets [GBJ25, Theorem 2.5,
     Corollary 2.6].
3. **Change detection** [SRR24, Definitions 2.2 and 2.6, Theorem 2.4].
   - Define e-detectors for a pre-change null: adapted processes with
     `∫⁻ M τ ∂P ≤ ∫⁻ τ ∂P` for every stopping time `τ` and every pre-change `P`. Prove that the
     first time an e-detector reaches `α⁻¹` has expectation at least `α⁻¹` under every pre-change
     law [SRR24, Theorem 2.4].
   - The Shiryaev–Roberts and CUSUM e-detectors, sums and maxima over `j` of e-processes started
     at time `j`, are e-detectors [SRR24, Definition 2.6].
4. **Two-sample and invariance testing by betting** [SR23, Theorem 1, Corollary 1; PBKR23,
   Theorem 2].
   - For an involution `T` of `𝓧`, observations whose conditional law given the past is
     `T`-invariant under the null, and payoffs $g_k(Z_k)-g_k(TZ_k)$ with $g_k$ predictable in a
     symmetric class of functions with values in `[-1/2, 1/2]`, betting on the payoffs with
     predictable bets in `[-1, 1]` is a test martingale for every prediction strategy, as a case of
     Layer 4.4 with the constraints `±(g(z) - g(T z))` [SR23, Theorem 1(a), Corollary 1].
   - For i.i.d. observations under the alternative, prove power one when the prediction regret is
     `o(n)` and below the separation, and, under the assumption (10) of [SR23], the lower bound on
     the growth rate with the Online Newton Step bettor (OnlineLearning 8.3) [SR23, Theorem 1]. The two-sample test is
     the swap of the two coordinates, and the kernelized independence test of [PBKR23, Theorem 2]
     an instance.

Generalization: invariance under any involution, where [SR23] treats the two-sample problem and
symmetry separately.

## Completion checks

- Every definition comes with measurability, monotonicity in the null and congruence lemmas; for
  a singleton null `{P}`, `IsEVar`, `IsTestSupermartingale` and `IsEProcess` unfold to the
  classical notions for `P`.
- No result listed as a corollary is proved before the statement it is a corollary of; in
  particular the likelihood-ratio, monotone-likelihood-ratio and Bernoulli numeraires come from
  Layer 1.2, the bounded, semi-bounded and heavy-tailed $\mathrm K_{\inf}$ formulas from Layer 1.4,
  Ville's inequality from the exact Ville identity, and e-BH, closed e-BH, online e-BH and stopped
  e-BH from the e-closure principles of Layer 8.1.
- The validity of every construction of Layers 2.1, 4 and 9 is stated for every member of a
  composite null and every stopping time, and Ville's inequality of Layer 3.2 yields its level-`α`
  sequential test.
- The constant process `1` is a test martingale and Ville's inequality is sharp for it at `c = 1`;
  the e-process `1` gives the trivial confidence sequence `Set.univ`.
- The exponential supermartingales of Identification 4.2, the betting martingale of
  OnlineLearning 16.4, and LMLExplore's time-uniform bounds (`Probability/Martingale/MGFBound.lean`,
  `Freedman.lean`, `SelfNormalized.lean`, `InformationTheory/LaplaceMixture.lean`) are test
  supermartingales for the corresponding composite nulls and confidence sequences through
  Layer 5.2, with no restatement of their proofs.
- For a simple null and a simple alternative, Layer 6 gives the rate $\mathrm{KL}(Q_1,P_1)$ and
  $\mathbb E_Q[\tau_\alpha]\sim\log(1/\alpha)/\mathrm{KL}(Q_1,P_1)$ of the sequential probability
  ratio test; for one mean constraint on `[0, 1]` it gives the rate `klBer` on Bernoulli
  alternatives.
- e-BH with a single hypothesis is the e-test at level `α`; the e-Closure Principle with the
  family-wise error loss recovers the classical closure principle; closed BH rejects at least the
  hypotheses BH rejects.
- Coin betting's optimality [Cle25, Theorem 3] is the case of one mean constraint of Layer 4.4,
  and [RR26, Theorem 3.14] the case of one alternative of Layer 6.2.
- `LMLExplore.predictorLikelihoodRatio` and its three theorems are derived from Layer 4.2, and
  Statlib's `IsEVar.prod` is the product case of Layer 0.1.

Compilation of the suggested signatures checks their types, not the truth of their admitted
theorems or the completion of these milestones.
